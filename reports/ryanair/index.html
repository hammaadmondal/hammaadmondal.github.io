Ryanair Holdings · RYA · Airlines · Initiation, 17 Nov 2026

Paying for a fuel trough that will not last

At €25.24 the shares imply a long-run operating margin of about 6%, under half what Ryanair earned in FY25–FY26. We think the FY28 fuel squeeze is a hedging-timing effect that fares will largely absorb.

Thesis

  • FY28 is a hedge roll-off, not a broken model. FY27 fuel is 80% hedged at about $67/bbl; FY28 only 25% at about $95/bbl. We forecast fuel per passenger +25% and the EBIT margin dipping to 6.6% in FY28, recovering to 11.1% in FY29.
  • Expensive fuel widens Ryanair's cost gap. Less-hedged rivals are cutting capacity and European short-haul supply stays tight, so we assume fares +9% in FY28, passing on about two-thirds of the fuel increase.
  • Growth and cash resume. MAX-10 deliveries from spring 2027 take traffic towards 300m by FY34; free cash flow rebuilds from €858m in FY28 to about €3.3bn by FY34, with €2.0bn of net cash today.

What the market is pricing in

Holding our explicit forecasts to FY34, the share price implies a long-run EBIT margin of 6.2%, against our 13% and a FY25–FY26 average of 13.5%. On trough FY28 earnings the stock trades at 24x; on our FY29 EPS of €1.86 it trades at 13.6x.

Valuation

The target blends a DCF (70%, WACC 8.84%, g 2.5%, explicit to FY34) with peer EV/EBITDA and P/E (15% each). Fair value of €29.51 is rolled forward one year at the 9.09% cost of equity, less dividends, to give the €31.81 target.

Risks

  • Fuel stays high (Hormuz, wide crack spreads): each 1% on FY28 fuel per passenger is about €78m of EBIT.
  • Fares fail to pass on fuel if consumers weaken.
  • MAX-10 certification or delivery delays.
  • EU environmental taxes, ATC disruption and regulatory fines.
  • Terminal value is 75% of enterprise value.
