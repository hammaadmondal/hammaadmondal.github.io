Anthropic, PBC · ANT · Nasdaq · IPO view · 19 Nov 2026

A great business at a full price

Anthropic's filings show $17.0bn of quarterly revenue, positive operating cash flow and customers that keep spending more. At $97.17 the market already pays about 19x run-rate revenue, so the stock needs about $300bn of revenue by 2030 just to earn a normal return.

My view: constructive on the business, neutral on the price

I would have wanted stock at the $83 IPO price. I would not chase it at today's close.

  • The business is real. Q3 2026 revenue was $17.0bn, up 1,127% year on year. Run-rate revenue reached about $81bn on 31 Oct 2026, and operations produced $6.6bn of cash in the first nine months.
  • The price already assumes years of success. Fully diluted equity value at the close is about $1.76tn. To earn 12% a year, revenue must reach about $300bn by 2030 if the shares then trade at 8x sales.
  • Day one was a warning, not a failure. The stock opened 34% above the IPO price and gave back about half of that gain to close up 17%. Anyone who bought at the open is already down 13%.

Day one: a big pop that faded

USD per share, US Eastern time

  • A late, hot open. The first trade came out of the opening auction at 11:48 a.m., more than two hours after the bell, at $111.50. Perpetual futures on Hyperliquid had traded around $105.50 at 10:30, so the open ran past even the most bullish pre-market marker.
  • A fast fade. The stock peaked at $114.99 a minute later, was near $95 within half an hour and hit the day's low of $92.72 at 1:18 p.m. It then traded between about $93 and $100 and closed at $97.17.
  • Heavy turnover on a small float. About 832m shares traded, 85% of the 980m sold. Only about 1.3bn shares could trade (IPO shares plus about 315m insider shares released on day one), roughly 10% of shares outstanding.
  • Money left on the table. The gap between $83 and the close is worth about $13.9bn on the IPO shares and $4.4bn on the private placement. That is the cost of a deal priced to work, and it buys goodwill for any future raise.

How I read it. The open was an allocation squeeze: institutions that got fewer shares than they asked for (Altimeter reportedly asked for $5bn) topped up at any price. The fade shows where long-term buyers think value starts. A close 17% above the offer says the deal was priced sensibly, not that the stock is cheap. The market helped: Nasdaq 100 futures were up about 2% before the open after a sharp AI sell-off the week before.

Lead banks: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, with about 20 others. Placement buyers: Google and NVIDIA (about $10bn each), AMD (about $2.5bn), Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft (about $1bn each) and TSMC (about $0.5bn); their shares are outside the underwriter lock-up.

Who is selling

Almost nobody. Existing holders sold 23.6m shares, about $2.0bn at $83. The rest is new money for compute and hiring. The IPO price is 41% above the Series H round (May 2026, $58.90 a share) and 3.2x Series G (Feb 2026, $25.91).

Who controls it

Not the public. Class A votes are capped at 49.9%. One Class F share held by the founders' LLC carries 50.1%, and one Class T share lets the Long-Term Benefit Trust elect a board majority. As a public benefit corporation, Anthropic weighs its safety mission alongside profit. Amazon and Google are set to own about 15% and 14%.

  • Q3 went backwards on GAAP profit. Q2 made $974m of operating income; Q3 lost $567m. The filing blames $5.0bn of costly short-term compute bought to meet demand, and about $600m of revenue lost to price cuts. Adjusted operating income still rose to $2.46bn (a 15% margin).
  • Customers are deep, not just wide. About 250 customers pay over $10m a year and 1,900 over $1m; net revenue retention is about 900%. The top five customers are about 25% of revenue, and 44% is billed through AWS, Google Cloud and Azure marketplaces. About 79% of revenue is usage-based, 21% subscriptions.
  • The obligation that matters. Committed compute and infrastructure spend is $900.9bn: $70.7bn due within 12 months and $451.6bn before the end of 2030. That averages about $106bn a year, more than today's $81bn run-rate. Cash and investments were $131.8bn at 30 Sep, against $6.2bn of debt.

Reverse valuation: what 2030 revenue does the price need?

Instead of forecasting, I ask what the price requires. In words: grow today's enterprise value at the return an investor wants for four years, then divide by the sales multiple a mature Anthropic might trade on in 2030.

  • Base case: about $300bn. That is 3.7x today's run-rate, or about 39% a year for four years. At a 30% operating margin, 8x sales is about 27x operating profit: full, but not extreme, for a dominant platform.
  • Is it plausible? Run-rate grew from about $9bn at end-2025 to $81bn ten months later, so 39% a year looks easy on trend. The harder part is price: Q3 already lost about $600m to price cuts, and each model generation gets cheaper. Volume has to outrun falling prices and rising compute at the same time.
  • My read. $300bn is achievable but not conservative. At $97 there is little margin of safety; at or below the $83 offer price, the maths starts to work.

Risks

  • Fixed compute costs. Broadcom (~$161bn), Google (≥$121bn), SpaceXAI (up to $121bn), Amazon (≥$110bn) and others; fixed if demand slows.
  • Circular ties. The main suppliers are also investors, distributors or anchor buyers.
  • Concentration. Top five customers ~25% of revenue; cloud marketplaces bill 44%.
  • Governance. Public holders cannot control the board.
  • Supply. About 3.6bn shares can unlock early in stages; today's float is ~10%.
  • Accounting noise. Fair-value charges swing net income by tens of billions; up to $42bn more convertible notes may go to Broadcom or a partner.

What would change my mind

Dates approximate

I would turn positive below about $85, or if Q4 shows compute back under 50% of revenue. I would turn negative if run-rate growth stalls before the big unlocks.
