Anthropic, PBC · ANT · Nasdaq · IPO view · updated 24 Nov 2026

The hype is gone. At $80, the price finally works

Anthropic's filings show $17.0bn of quarterly revenue, positive operating cash flow and customers that keep spending more. In four sessions the stock went from $115 to below its $83 IPO price. At $80 it needs about $240bn of revenue by 2030 to earn a normal return, against $300bn at the day-one close.

My view: positive, as a patient buyer below $85

My earlier versions said I would turn positive below about $85. On 24 Nov ANT fell below its $83 IPO price, so the trigger has been hit. I would buy in stages, because large share unlocks start on 15 Dec.

  • The business is unchanged and strong. Q3 2026 revenue was $17.0bn, up 1,127% year on year. Run-rate revenue reached about $81bn on 31 Oct 2026, and operations produced $6.6bn of cash in the first nine months.
  • The price now asks for much less. At $80, fully diluted value is about $1.46tn, or 15x run-rate revenue after cash. To earn 12% a year, revenue must reach about $240bn by 2030 at 8x sales: about 31% growth a year.
  • The fall was mostly not about Anthropic's numbers. No new company data came out. The drivers were an AI sell-off, the end of bank support, early-holder selling, worries about cheaper open-weight models and a heavy unlock calendar. Those weigh on the price for weeks, not on the business for years.

Day one (19 Nov)

  • A late, hot open. The first trade came out of the opening auction at 11:48 a.m., more than two hours after the bell, at $111.50. Perpetual futures on Hyperliquid had traded around $105.50 at 10:30.
  • A fast fade. The stock peaked at $115.10, was near $95 within half an hour and hit $92.72 at 1:18 p.m. It closed at $97.17.
  • Heavy turnover on a small float. About 832m shares traded, 85% of the 980m sold. Only about 1.3bn shares could trade, roughly 10% of shares outstanding.

Day two (20 Nov)

  • Gap down at the open. ANT opened at $89.86, already 7.5% below Thursday's close, after pre-market trading near $85.
  • Five cents from the IPO price. The low of $83.05 came at 12:49 p.m. Buyers stepped in there and the stock closed at $88.91, down 8.5%.
  • Mostly the market, partly the deal. The Nasdaq fell about 5.3% that day and about 10% over the week, its worst week since 2020. Nvidia fell for a seventh straight day amid turmoil after Jensen Huang's resignation, and a threatened tax on US companies building data centres abroad hit AI names. On the same day, the full greenshoe exercise ended the banks' price support.

Monday (23 Nov)

  • Left out of the rally. ANT opened at $93.69 and closed at $87.64, down 1.4%, while the S&P 500 rose 4.7% and the Nasdaq Composite 6.5% after Nvidia brought back Jensen Huang as CEO.
  • New instruments. Listed options and several 2x leveraged Anthropic ETFs started trading.
  • Retail kept buying. Individual investors bought about $100m more ANT than they sold on both Friday and Monday (Vanda Research).

Tuesday (24 Nov), morning

  • Below issue. ANT traded below $83 pre-market, fell as low as $79.42 just after the open and was around $78–80 in mid-morning, down 9–11%. Nvidia (−7%), CoreWeave and Nebius (−8% each) also fell as the AI trade gave back Monday's gains.
  • First broker coverage, all positive. Canaccord Genuity started at Buy ($135), Oppenheimer at Outperform ($150) and Wedbush at Outperform ($120).
  • A new bear argument. Moor Insights' Patrick Moorhead said he would sell, arguing that Huang's return will push Nvidia's open-weight models harder against Anthropic.

How I read the first week. Day one's open was an allocation squeeze: roughly half of the institutions that ordered stock got none, and some topped up at any price. Since then the stock has fallen on weak AI days and failed to rise on strong ones. That is what early-holder selling looks like once bank support has ended. One investor called it “the real price discovery”, and I agree. The difference now is the price: at $80 the stock asks for about $240bn of 2030 revenue, a bar I think Anthropic can clear.

Lead banks: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, with about 20 others. Placement buyers: Google and NVIDIA (about $10bn each), AMD (about $2.5bn), Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft (about $1bn each) and TSMC (about $0.5bn); their shares are outside the underwriter lock-up.

Why the greenshoe matters

Morgan Stanley, the stabilisation agent, exercised the full 147m-share option on 20 Nov. With its short position covered, the bank no longer buys in the market when the price falls. That support ended on day two, the same day the stock nearly touched $83.

Who is selling

Almost nobody. The seven co-founders sold 23.6m shares in the deal, 3,378,378 each, including Dario and Daniela Amodei (about $280m each). Director Christopher Liddell bought about $5m of stock at $83. At $80 the stock is still 36% above the Series H round (May 2026, $58.90 a share).

Who controls it

Not the public. Class A votes are capped at 49.9%. One Class F share held by the founders' LLC carries 50.1%, and one Class T share lets the Long-Term Benefit Trust elect a board majority. As a public benefit corporation, Anthropic weighs its safety mission alongside profit.

Who owns it

Amazon and Google are set to own about 15% and 14% after the deal. Both are also suppliers of compute to Anthropic and resellers of its models through their clouds.

  • Q3 went backwards on GAAP profit. Q2 made $974m of operating income; Q3 lost $567m. The filing blames $5.0bn of costly short-term compute bought to meet demand, and about $600m of revenue lost to price cuts. Adjusted operating income still rose to $2.46bn (a 15% margin).
  • Customers are deep, not just wide. About 250 customers pay over $10m a year and 1,900 over $1m; net revenue retention is about 900%. The top five customers are about 25% of revenue, and 44% is billed through AWS, Google Cloud and Azure marketplaces. About 79% of revenue is usage-based, 21% subscriptions.
  • The obligation that matters. Committed compute and infrastructure spend is $900.9bn: $70.7bn due within 12 months and $451.6bn before the end of 2030. That averages about $106bn a year, more than today's $81bn run-rate. Cash and investments were $131.8bn at 30 Sep, against $6.2bn of debt.

Reverse valuation: what 2030 revenue does the price need?

Instead of forecasting, I ask what the price requires. In words: grow today's enterprise value at the return an investor wants for four years, then divide by the sales multiple a mature Anthropic might trade on in 2030.

  • Base case: about $240bn. That is 3.0x today's run-rate, or about 31% a year for four years.
  • Is it plausible? Run-rate grew from about $9bn at end-2025 to $81bn ten months later, so 31% a year is well below recent growth. The risks are price and cost: Q3 lost about $600m to price cuts, open-weight models keep getting cheaper and compute rose to 58% of revenue.
  • My read. $240bn is more likely than not, which is why I now see the price as attractive. The brokers' $120–150 targets imply far more; I would not lean on them.

Risks

  • Fixed compute costs. Broadcom (~$161bn), Google (≥$121bn), SpaceXAI (up to $121bn), Amazon (≥$110bn) and others; fixed if demand slows.
  • Open-weight competition. DeepSeek, Mistral, Moonshot AI and Nvidia's Nemotron could pressure prices.
  • Share supply. Unlocks of up to 303m (15 Dec), 303m (5 Jan), 353m (19 Jan) and 353m (2 Feb), then about 1.7bn after FY2026 results and about 3bn later.
  • Trading risk. ANT falls with the AI sector but has not joined its rallies; options and 2x ETFs can amplify swings.
  • Circular ties. The main suppliers are also investors, distributors or anchor buyers.
  • Concentration and governance. Top five customers ~25% of revenue; public holders cannot control the board.

What would change my mind

Dates approximate

I would turn neutral again above about $95 without new data, or negative if Q4 run-rate growth stalls or compute stays above 55% of revenue. I would add more if the stock holds $80 through the December unlocks.
