A high-return bank, priced for it

AIB earns well above its cost of equity and returns almost all of it to shareholders. At €11.50 the shares imply a sustainable return on tangible equity of about 17%, almost exactly our base case: attractive income, limited re-rating.

Thesis

  • Rate hikes have rebuilt net interest income. The ECB deposit rate rose from 2.00% to 2.50% in 2026; with loans up about 5.5% we forecast NII of €3.87bn in 2026 and €4.15bn in 2027.
  • A capital-return machine. A 40–60% cash payout plus buybacks above the 14% CET1 target: about €2.6bn of distributions in 2027, roughly 11% of market value.
  • But the market already pays for it. 10.9x 2027E earnings against a 10.4x peer median, and 2.0x tangible book against 1.9x. Base upside of +10% (about 15% total return) is fair, not compelling.

What the market is pricing in

Turning the justified price-to-tangible-book formula round, the price implies a sustainable ROTE of 17.1%, against our 17% base, 14% bear and 19% bull. Overweight needs returns to stay near today's 19–21% after rates normalise.

Valuation

A bank is valued on returns and capital, not EBITDA. The target blends an excess-capital dividend model (40%) and justified P/TBV (30%) with peer P/E and P/TBV (15% each), at a 9.14% cost of equity. Fair value of €12.14 rolled forward one year, less dividends, gives €12.69.

Risks

  • Rates fall: each 100bp is about €256m of net interest income.
  • Irish economy, commercial property and SME credit.
  • Mortgage price competition.
  • Regulation of capital, levies or distributions.
  • Upside: further hikes, special distributions, Strategy 2030 targets (March 2027).
