Reverse DCF

A normal DCF asks what a company is worth. A reverse DCF starts from the share price and asks what you have to believe to pay it. Enter the price and a few numbers from the accounts, and the tool solves for the revenue growth, or the cash margin, the market is implying.

How it works

The tool runs a simple DCF and searches for the input that makes the DCF value equal to today's enterprise value.

  • Enterprise value today. Market cap plus net debt: what you pay for the whole business, debt included.
  • Cash flow each year. Last year's revenue, grown at the growth rate for that many years, times the cash margin.
$$FCF_t = R_0\,(1+g)^t \times m$$
  • Value of the business. Each year's cash flow divided by one plus the discount rate raised to that year, added up, plus a terminal value discounted the same way.
$$EV = \sum_{t=1}^{N} \frac{FCF_t}{(1+WACC)^t} + \frac{TV_N}{(1+WACC)^N}$$
  • Terminal value. Next year's cash flow divided by the gap between the discount rate and the long-run growth rate.
$$TV_N = \frac{FCF_N\,(1+g_{T})}{WACC - g_{T}}$$
  • The reverse step. Solving for growth: the tool tries growth rates (bisection) until the DCF value equals market EV to within 0.0001%. Solving for margin is direct, because value rises one-for-one with the margin.
$$m^{*} = \frac{EV_{market}}{EV\ \text{at}\ m = 100\%}$$
  • Simplifications. Growth and margin are held flat for the forecast; cash flows arrive at year end; there is no stub period. My full models use year-by-year forecasts, so their answers will differ. Use this for a quick read on expectations, not as a price target.
